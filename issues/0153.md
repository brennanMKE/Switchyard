## Description

The half of the original #0042 that needs the journal store: take the
`ReferenceTransaction.Decision.updates` a foreign `committed` transaction produced (#0042's
decision core) and persist them as **observed** journal entries — distinct from undoable ones —
in the metadata store #0027/#0028 build. This is what lets the guard say *what* moved a ref
between two `switchyard` commands instead of merely that something did.

## Expected behavior

- [ ] A committed foreign transaction's updates land as an observed entry (operation, refs with
      old/new values, timestamp, worktree), marked so `undo` never offers to revert it.
- [ ] The journal's own ref writes never produce an entry (the #0042 marker path, exercised end
      to end through the store).
- [ ] Failure to persist never surfaces as a non-zero hook exit — the #0042 totality invariant
      holds through this layer too.
- [ ] `swift test` prints a `Test run with N tests` line; N must exceed the pre-change baseline
      (re-measure at planning time; state the delta).

## Planning note — 2026-08-09. A design fork that needs Brennan's call.

**This issue cannot be implemented without deciding where observed entries live**, and the two
options produce materially different work. Recording both rather than picking.

### The constraint that creates the fork

`undo` must never offer to revert an observed entry. But #0155 decision 2 is explicit: **an entry's
kind is decided by the presence of `traversal`, never by matching the `operation` string.** There is
no third kind today, and `JournalChain` treats every non-traversal entry as an undo target
(#0166). So "observed" needs a representation, and `operation: "observed"` is exactly what the
design forbids.

### Option A — a new optional field on the entry metadata

Add `observed: ObservedTransaction?` to `JournalEntryMetadata`, mirroring `traversal`: presence
marks the kind.

- Consistent with the established idiom, and observed entries stay in the journal namespace, so
  **#0030 recovers them from refs alone** like everything else.
- But it changes a **wire format pinned by golden-bytes tests** (#0155, #0179), and `chainNode`
  must become optional or gain a sibling — it has **four call sites** (`JournalRestore`,
  `JournalUndo`, `JournalList` ×2). `JournalList` also needs a `ChainPosition` case so observed
  entries list with a position rather than reading as defective.
- Realistically two issues: the metadata/chain change, then this one composing on it.

### Option B — a separate ref namespace

Write observed entries under `refs/switchyard/observed/` rather than the journal namespace.

- No wire change, no `chainNode` change, no new `ChainPosition`. They cannot appear on the chain
  because they are not in the journal's ref space at all — the strongest possible form of "undo
  never offers them".
- But `JournalRebuild` scans `JournalAnchor.refPrefix`, so **observed entries are not recovered by
  a rebuild** unless it learns a second namespace. Whether that matters is the real question: they
  are a record of *foreign* activity, and losing them on a rebuild may be acceptable — but that is
  a decision, not an oversight to discover later.
- `RefSnapshot` already filters the whole `refs/switchyard/` namespace, so capture/restore is
  unaffected either way.

### What I would recommend, and why it is still your call

**Option B**, on the grounds that it makes the safety property structural rather than enforced by
four call sites agreeing — and #0157 just demonstrated how easily a filter that must be applied
everywhere gets missed. The cost is that rebuild recovery becomes a deliberate choice rather than a
free consequence.

Not started. Blocked on this decision.
